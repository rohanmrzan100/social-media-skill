# Retention Script — structure for staying watched

> **Expectations vs. Reality.** A viewer keeps watching only while the *reality* of the content keeps exceeding the *expectation* set by the title/hook. The instant reality drops below expectation, they leave. Every structural choice below exists to keep reality above the line — start to finish.

This is the **full-script structural layer**. `hook-machine` engineers the single hook *line*; this skill arranges the whole piece — idea, intro, body order, value per point, outro — so attention doesn't decay. It's framed around long-form, but every step ports to short-form (notes inline).

## When to consult this skill

- Writing, structuring, or fixing a **full script** — or its outline, intro, body, or outro.
- "Why do viewers drop off halfway?" / retention or pacing problems.
- Need **point ordering**, a **value loop**, **re-hooks**, or a **non-disruptive CTA**.

**Do not use for:** the standalone hook wording → `hook-machine`; shooting → `solo-reel-shoot`.

## Core thesis (read first)

You are managing one gap the whole time: **expectation set vs. value delivered.** Beat it continuously. Two failure modes: (1) the title over-promises and reality can't match → drop-off; (2) the content is just common knowledge → reality never *exceeds* expectation → boredom. Step 2 (Outline) defends against #2 before you write a word.

## The 5-step framework

### 1. Packaging — idea, title, thumbnail (do this *first*)
Define the **idea**, **title**, and **thumbnail** before scripting, because they set the expectation everything else must beat. The opening must deliver **click confirmation**: the first lines immediately confirm the promise the title made, so the viewer knows they're in the right place.
*Short-form:* title/thumbnail → the cover frame + on-screen title + first 1–2 seconds. Confirm the promise instantly or they skip.

### 2. Outline — audit for novelty *before* the intro
Write a **bulleted list of your points first**. Then audit: is each point novel/non-obvious, or common knowledge? **Common knowledge cannot beat expectation.** Cut or sharpen anything obvious until it earns its place. (Same instinct as `fandom-funnel`'s "non-obvious insights" and `hook-machine`'s specificity lever.)

### 3. Intro — the structured hook (4 beats)
1. **Immediate context** — state plainly what the video is about (click confirmation).
2. **Common belief** — name what the viewer already thinks; connect.
3. **Contrarian approach** — challenge that belief; open the curiosity gap.
4. **Proof / plan** — establish credibility and set the expectation for what's coming.

> Run `hook-machine` on beat 1's actual wording — this step gives the *structure*; hook-machine sharpens the *line* against real data.

### 4. Body — order and value
- **2-1-3-4 ordering:** lead with your **second-best** point, then your best, then the rest. Opening strong (but not strongest) then escalating builds a subconscious pattern of *increasing* value that holds attention.
- **Value loop per point** — every point delivers three beats:
  - **Context** (what it is)
  - **Application** (how to use it)
  - **Framing** (why it matters)
  - *Maps to `fandom-funnel`'s 3-2-1 transformation — leave them changed, not just informed.*
- **Re-hook between points:** a mini-hook on each transition to prevent attention decay and tee up the next point. Never let a point just *end* — bridge to the next.
- **Story loops (open → delay → close):** the brain is a prediction machine and hates an unresolved gap. Open a loop — a question, a stake, a "wait for it" — *before* you deliver the answer, so the viewer has to stay for closure. The re-hook above is one loop closing as the next opens; this is the underlying mechanic.
  - **Loop stacking** (long-form only): keep a *primary* loop (the title's promise) open across the whole piece while opening and closing smaller secondary loops inside the wait, so the open-gap count never drops to zero.
- **"But / Therefore," never "and then":** connect beats with **But** (conflict) or **Therefore** (consequence) — never a flat "and then," which has no momentum. If two sentences only join with "and then," one of them isn't earning its place. This is the sentence-level version of keeping reality above expectation.

### 5. Outro — the "fortune cookie"
Short, high-value close: recap the key takeaways and remind the viewer of the value they got. That reminder is what earns the like / share / rewatch — don't beg for it, *justify* it.

## Native CTA embedding

Don't bolt a CTA on. **Proactively find the point that naturally benefits from your resource/product**, and embed the CTA there so it reads as a *helpful extension* of the content, not an interruption. The best ad is the one the viewer would have asked for at that exact moment.

## One caution

The exact devices — **2-1-3-4 ordering, the 4-beat intro, "fortune cookie," loop stacking** — are **heuristics, not laws**; they're tuned for talking-head long-form and should bend for a 15-second Reel. In particular, **a short Reel has room for one open loop, maybe a nested second — do not stack loops on short-form**; that scaffolding is built for an 8–20 min retention curve, not 20–40 seconds. The durable, transferable ideas are: **keep reality above expectation, audit for novelty before writing, escalate value through the body, open a loop before you close one, connect beats with But/Therefore not "and then," and embed CTAs where they're genuinely useful.** Lead with those.
